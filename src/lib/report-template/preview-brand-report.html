Executive Summary

High-level assessment and priority actions.

Acme's brand identity materials show a foundation of professional design, but lack the consistency and clarity needed to build strong brand recognition. The visual system is fragmented across materials, with different color palettes, typography choices, and layout patterns appearing in the pitch deck, website mockups, and marketing collateral.

The messaging relies heavily on industry jargon and feature descriptions rather than benefit-driven language. The value proposition is present but buried beneath technical details that don't resonate with the stated target audience of mid-market decision-makers.

The highest-impact improvements are: unifying the visual identity system with a single colour palette and type stack, rewriting the core value proposition to lead with outcomes, and establishing a tone-of-voice guide that ensures consistent messaging across all touchpoints.

Detailed Findings

All issues ranked by severity, with recommendations and expected impact.

Visual Consistency 3 findings
Critical Visual Consistency
Three different primary blue colours used across materials
The pitch deck uses #2563EB, the website mockup uses #3B82F6, and the brochure uses #1E40AF. This fragmentation prevents brand recognition and signals a lack of design governance. Users encountering different materials won't associate them with the same brand.
Recommendation
Define a single primary blue with exact hex, RGB, and CMYK values. Document it in a brand colour guide with usage rules for backgrounds, text, and accents.
Expected impact
Immediate visual cohesion across all materials. Stronger brand recognition and professional appearance.
High Visual Consistency
Competing typefaces weaken visual identity
Three typefaces are used across materials: Poppins in the pitch deck, Inter on the website, and Roboto in the brochure. This creates a disjointed reading experience and undermines the brand's visual system.
Recommendation
Select one typeface family (Inter or DM Sans work well at all sizes) and use weight variations for hierarchy. Document the type scale with specific sizes for H1-H4 and body text.
Medium Visual Consistency
Logo placement varies between centred and left-aligned
The logo appears centred on the pitch deck title slide but left-aligned on all website pages and in the brochure header. While minor, this inconsistency compounds with the colour and type issues to create a fragmented brand impression.
Recommendation
Establish clear logo placement rules: left-aligned for documents and web, centred for standalone/hero uses. Include minimum clear space and sizing guidelines.
Tone of Voice & Messaging 2 findings
High Tone of Voice
Tone shifts between overly technical and casually informal
The pitch deck uses enterprise language ("event-driven microservices architecture") while the homepage says "super easy to set up". These register shifts confuse the audience about who the product is for and undermine credibility with both technical and business audiences.
Recommendation
Create a tone-of-voice spectrum document: professional but approachable. Define "we say / we don't say" examples. Aim for language that a VP of Engineering and a Head of Operations both find credible.
Medium Tone of Voice
No consistent messaging framework across touchpoints
Each material has its own headline, tagline, and supporting copy that don't reference a shared messaging hierarchy. The pitch deck opens with "Transform your operations" while the website says "Built for modern teams" and the brochure leads with "Enterprise-grade reliability".
Recommendation
Build a messaging house: one master positioning statement, 3 supporting pillars, and specific proof points for each. All materials should draw from this framework.
