Executive Summary

High-level assessment and priority actions.

The Acme Corp brand materials demonstrate a solid foundation with consistent use of the primary colour palette and typography across most documents. The brand voice is generally professional and appropriate for the target B2B audience.

Key areas for improvement include strengthening the competitive positioning with clearer differentiation language, tightening the value proposition to be more specific about outcomes, and addressing minor inconsistencies in tone between the sales deck and the brand guidelines document.

The overall production quality is good, though several documents would benefit from tighter formatting discipline and more rigorous proofreading of secondary materials like email templates and social media copy.

Detailed Findings

All issues ranked by severity, with recommendations and expected impact.

Value Proposition Strength 2 findings
High Value Proposition Strength
Value proposition lacks quantified outcomes
The primary value proposition "Helping businesses grow" appears across the brand deck and website copy. This is indistinguishable from thousands of competitors and does not communicate specific, measurable outcomes.
Recommendation
Reframe with a specific outcome: e.g., "Reduces onboarding time by 60% for mid-market SaaS teams." Include proof points from case studies.
Expected Impact
Quantified value propositions convert 2-3x better in B2B sales materials according to industry benchmarks.
Medium Value Proposition Strength
Benefits are listed as features rather than outcomes
The feature list in the sales deck uses technical specifications instead of customer-centric benefits. "AI-powered analytics" does not tell the reader what they gain.
Recommendation
Rewrite each feature as a benefit: "AI-powered analytics" becomes "Spot revenue leaks in real time without a data team."
Tone of Voice & Messaging 2 findings
High Tone of Voice & Messaging
Inconsistent tone between sales deck and brand guidelines
The sales deck uses a casual, first-person tone ("We're here to help you crush it") while the brand guidelines specify a formal, third-person voice ("Acme Corp delivers enterprise solutions"). This creates confusion about the brand's identity.
Recommendation
Choose one voice register and document it with examples in the brand guidelines. Update the sales deck to match.
Low Tone of Voice & Messaging
Overuse of industry jargon in customer-facing materials
Terms like "synergistic ecosystem" and "paradigm-shifting" appear in the brand deck without context or definition.
Recommendation
Replace jargon with plain language. Test copy with someone outside the industry for clarity.
Competitive Positioning 1 finding
Medium Competitive Positioning
No competitive differentiation framework in sales materials
The sales deck lists features but does not position them relative to competitors. Prospects have no way to understand why Acme is different from alternatives.
Recommendation
Add a comparison matrix or "Why Acme" section that visually maps your strengths against the top 2-3 alternatives.
Expected Impact
Comparison frameworks reduce sales cycle length by giving prospects the information they need to decide without additional research.

Visual Consistency 1 finding
Medium Visual Consistency
Secondary colour used inconsistently across documents
The brand guidelines define a secondary blue (#2563EB), but the sales deck uses a lighter variant (#60A5FA) and the email templates use yet another shade (#3B82F6).
Recommendation
Consolidate to one secondary blue value. Distribute a colour swatch file (ASE or CSS variables) to all teams creating branded materials.
Professionalism & Polish 2 findings
Medium Professionalism & Polish
Formatting inconsistencies in the sales deck
Three slides use different heading sizes (28pt, 32pt, 36pt) and two slides have misaligned text boxes. The footer date on slide 12 still says "2024".
Recommendation
Apply the master slide template consistently. Set up a pre-send checklist for date references and alignment.
Low Professionalism & Polish
Minor grammatical errors in email templates
Two email templates contain subject-verb agreement errors and one has a duplicated paragraph.
Recommendation
Run all templates through a copy review process before deployment. Consider adding automated grammar checks to the workflow.
Wording Quality 1 finding
