# Fixpath Marketing Site Revamp Brief

This brief defines the full direction for revamping the Fixpath marketing site. The current live site contains a lot of valuable information, but it is spread across the page in a way that makes it hard for a new visitor to immediately understand who Fixpath is, what it does, how it works, why it is different, and what kind of company it is. The new site must feel clean, structured, polished, and unmistakably clear within the first few seconds. It must also reflect the actual standards Fixpath applies to customer websites, because the product should demonstrate the same truth, trust, usefulness, clarity, accessibility, and technical quality that it evaluates in others. [cite:512][cite:462]

## Core objective

We are not rebuilding the marketing site just to make it prettier. We are rebuilding it so the site becomes a clear, high-trust product explanation system.

The site should immediately communicate:

- who Fixpath is,
- what Fixpath does,
- how it works,
- why it is different,
- who it is for,
- who it is not for,
- what happens after an audit,
- and why users should trust it.

The current problem is not lack of content. The current problem is weak structure, weak prioritization, and weak clarity.

## Positioning

Fixpath should not be positioned as “just audit your website.”

The positioning is:

- truth,
- trust,
- usefulness,
- a decision engine for real website and brand issues,
- a prioritization system,
- a fix guidance system,
- a progress tracking system,
- and a trust-building product. [cite:512][cite:471]

This framing should shape the entire site.

## Brand and tone direction

The site should feel:

- elite but simple,
- precise but not cold,
- modern but not trendy,
- visual but not flashy,
- structured but not corporate-heavy,
- and calm, crisp, and Series A-level in execution. [cite:471][cite:513]

Reference feel:
- Cursor-level restraint
- Perplexity-level clarity
- strong product confidence
- no startup clichés
- no bloated copy
- no vague AI promises [cite:471][cite:513]

## Site principles

### 1. Clarity in under five seconds

A visitor landing on the homepage should understand the product category and promise almost immediately.

### 2. Visual structure over long explanation

Do not solve clarity problems by adding more paragraphs. Solve them with stronger layout, hierarchy, sectioning, icons, screenshots, diagrams, cards, and tighter copy. [cite:512]

### 3. Eat our own cookie

The marketing site must score well according to the same standards Fixpath audits:

- clear value proposition,
- strong content hierarchy,
- strong trust signals,
- strong readability,
- strong technical hygiene,
- strong discoverability,
- and strong accessibility readiness.

We should not market an engine for clarity, trust, and useful fixes while the website itself feels unclear or weakly structured.

### 4. SEO and AI readability by design

The site must be easy for both humans and retrieval systems to understand.

That means:
- stable heading hierarchy,
- clear page intent,
- strong semantic structure,
- concise section-level messaging,
- internal links with intent,
- obvious entity clarity,
- and low-noise copy.

### 5. Product truth over hype

Do not overclaim. Do not say Fixpath does everything. Do not market fake certainty.

The site should communicate confidence through precision.

## Homepage

The homepage is the most important page and must do the heaviest clarity work.

### Homepage goal

Answer, in order:

1. What is Fixpath?
2. What problem does it solve?
3. How does it work?
4. Why is it different from generic audit tools?
5. What does the user get after the audit?
6. Why should I trust this?
7. What should I do next?

### Homepage structure

#### 1. Header

Keep it minimal and high-trust.

Navigation:
- Product
- How it Works
- Why Fixpath
- Pricing
- About
- Resources
- Sign in
- CTA: Start audit / Book demo

Header should be calm and compact.

#### 2. Hero

This is the most important clarity block on the whole site.

Hero must communicate:
- Fixpath helps companies identify real website and brand issues,
- prioritize what matters,
- guide fixes,
- and track progress over time.

Hero should not be generic “AI-powered website audit” language.

Recommended hero content structure:
- short eyebrow
- strong headline
- one short supporting paragraph
- primary CTA
- secondary CTA
- product UI visual / screenshot / motion block

Hero message direction:
- Find what is hurting trust.
- Fix what matters.
- Track what improves.

or

- A decision engine for real website and brand issues.

or

- See what is actually hurting trust, clarity, and growth — then fix it.

The hero visual should show the product doing real work: findings, priorities, fixes, progress, score movement.

#### 3. Trust strip

Short visual strip below hero.

Purpose:
- reduce skepticism fast,
- show product maturity,
- show credibility without long explanation.

Could include:
- product principles,
- customer type badges,
- “built for teams who need real fixes, not noise,”
- proof signals if available.

#### 4. What Fixpath is

A short, structured section that explains Fixpath in product terms.

Use 4 or 5 visual cards for the core product identity:
- decision engine
- prioritization system
- fix guidance
- progress tracking
- trust-building layer

This section should be highly visual and low-copy.

#### 5. How it works

Show the actual Find → Fix → Track flow visually.

Recommended structure:
- Find: detect real issues
- Fix: understand what matters and how to fix it
- Track: verify progress over time

This should be one of the cleanest sections on the entire site.

Use screenshots, flow steps, arrows, or a horizontal product narrative.

#### 6. What makes us different

This is where the audit bible philosophy becomes a market differentiator.

Fixpath is different because it is not trying to always find something.
It is built around truth, trust, and usefulness.

This section should highlight ideas like:
- real issues over endless findings,
- prioritization over noise,
- fix guidance over generic reports,
- progress tracking over one-off audits,
- truth over inflated scoring.

This is strategically important because it reframes the category.

#### 7. Product proof section

Show what users actually get.

Use a strong visual layout with 3–5 focused proof cards:
- prioritized findings
- fix recommendations
- impact explanation
- accessibility / trust / technical categories
- re-audit / tracking progress

Show the product, not abstract promises. [cite:463]

#### 8. Who it is for / who it is not for

This is important and often missing.

Use a clean two-column section.

For example:
- for teams that want clarity and action,
- for brands that care about trust and consistency,
- for operators who want real fixes,
- not for people looking for vanity dashboards,
- not for endless SEO checklist noise,
- not for generic reporting layers.

This sharpens positioning and improves trust.

#### 9. Final CTA

Simple and clean.

Not a long footer sermon.

One clear action.

### Key requirement

The Product page should show that Fixpath is not just an auditing tool. It is the full system from issue detection to fix guidance to progress verification. [cite:466][cite:512]

## How it Works page

### Goal

Make the product flow obvious.

### Sections

1. Start an audit
2. Site mapping / analysis
3. Real issues detected and prioritized
4. Fix guidance generated
5. Progress tracked through re-audit
6. Team workflow / operator workflow

This page should be diagram-first, not essay-first.

The user should understand the product mechanics without reading too much.

## Why Fixpath page

### Goal

Own the category difference.

This page should explain:
- why most audits feel noisy,
- why users stop trusting scores,
- why repeated findings kill credibility,
- and why Fixpath is built differently.

This is where the audit-bible logic becomes marketing language.

Sections:
1. The problem with generic audits
2. What teams actually need
3. The Fixpath philosophy: truth, trust, usefulness
4. How we report real issues
5. Why tracking matters more than one-time reports
6. CTA

This page is very important for strategic positioning.

## Solutions / Use Cases page

### Goal

Help buyers see themselves in the product.

Recommended sections:
- For in-house marketing teams
- For agencies
- For product/brand teams
- For websites with trust/conversion problems
- For brands preparing redesigns or migrations

Keep each block structured and concise.

### Goal

Make the decision easier, not harder.

Requirements:
- simple plans,
- clear feature comparison,
- low friction,
- strong FAQ,
- no clutter.

Also include product confidence points:
- what is included,
- how audits work,
- what happens after an audit,
- what “deep audit” means,
- how tracking works.

## About page

### Goal

Humanize the company without becoming self-indulgent.

This page should explain:
- why Fixpath exists,
- what problem it believes the market has,
- the values behind the product,
- and what kind of product company this is.

Important message:
Fixpath is not trying to be another generic reporting tool. It exists because too many teams get noise instead of useful truth.

## Resources / Blog / Guides

### Goal

Support SEO and AI readability with genuinely useful content.

Recommended content types:
- guides on trust, clarity, accessibility, and content quality
- website audit education
- explainers on re-audits and progress tracking
- examples of real issues vs noise
- category-specific practical content

Important rule:
Resources should be structured, scannable, and high-signal. Not AI fluff.

## Contact / Demo page

### Goal

Simple conversion.

Requirements:
- clear CTA,
- low-friction form,
- clear reason to reach out,
- trust language,
- no clutter.

## Section-level content guidance

Across the site, sections should follow this pattern:

- one job per section,
- one main visual or structure,
- one clear idea,
- one strong heading,
- short supporting copy,
- obvious next step.

Do not build sections that try to explain five things at once.

## Copy rules

### Do

- keep copy tight,
- write with confidence,
- be specific,
- use short paragraphs,
- use strong section titles,
- write for both people and retrieval systems,
- use repetition only for key narrative anchors like Find / Fix / Track.

### Don’t

- do not write long startup paragraphs,
- do not say “AI-powered” everywhere,
- do not use vague transformation language,
- do not overexplain obvious things,
- do not hide the product behind buzzwords,
- do not sound like a generic SEO SaaS.

## Design and UI direction

The site should feel like a polished product company site, not a landing-page template.

### Visual direction

- strong whitespace,
- restrained color,
- sharp typography,
- modular sections,
- great screenshots,
- subtle motion,
- minimal ornament.

### Avoid

- glowing AI visuals,
- gradient spam,
- overloaded cards,
- giant text walls,
- decorative nonsense,
- 2019 SaaS clichés,
- dense feature dumps. [cite:471][cite:513]

### Important

The product UI shown on the site must be clean and believable. It should reinforce clarity, not create more noise. [cite:463]

## SEO and AI readability requirements

Claude must ensure the new site is structurally strong for search and AI retrieval.

### Required standards

- one clear intent per page,
- strong H1/H2 hierarchy,
- descriptive titles and meta descriptions,
- concise page-level summaries,
- semantic HTML,
- strong internal linking,
- clean FAQs where useful,
- structured content blocks,
- accessible link text,
- image alt text,
- schema where appropriate,
- fast load,
- low layout noise.

### Important content rule

Every page should be answerable as a standalone entity.
A retrieval system should be able to tell what that page is about without ambiguity.

## Accessibility requirements

Because Fixpath evaluates accessibility readiness, the site should be a positive example.

Claude must ensure:
- strong contrast,
- heading hierarchy,
- proper labels,
- keyboard usability,
- semantic landmarks,
- alt text,
- button/link clarity,
- readable spacing,
- and no accessibility hypocrisy.
